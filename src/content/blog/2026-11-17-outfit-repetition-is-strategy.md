I used to apologize for outfit repeats.

"Sorry, wore this blazer Tuesday." As if my colleague tracked my Tuesday blazer. As if anyone tracks anyone's Tuesday blazer.

The apology wasn't for them. It was for me. Proof that I still had "options." That I wasn't *that person* who wears the same thing.

Then I counted.

Navy blazer: 42 wears this year. Cream merino: 38 wears. Straight-leg dark denim: 55 wears. White sneakers: 60 wears.

The pieces I apologized for? They were my *most worn*. The pieces I bought for "variety"? Charcoal wool blend blazer: 3 wears. Rust chunky knit: 2 wears. That $180 silk blouse: 1 wear.

The math was embarrassing. $2,800 in impulse purchases. 43 hours staring at a 134-item closet. Decision fatigue before 8 AM every single day.

## The Pivot

Sunday night. Phone camera. Ten minutes scanning hanging sections, drawer by drawer.

The app detected 134 pieces. Connected email — 23 more from two years of receipts. Connected calendar — nine distinct occasion slots this week alone.

Then the formulas emerged from actual wear data, not aspiration:

**WFH grocery run** — merino tee, joggers, white sneakers. Worn 3x this week.
**Rain commute + office** — trench, merino base, straight-leg jeans, ankle boots. Saved Tuesday.
**Client call anchor** — navy blazer, merino v-neck, dark denim, ankle boots. Tuesday AND Thursday.
**Travel walking day** — rain shell, merino tee, stretch chinos, white sneakers. Packed Thursday.
**Date night** — rust chunky knit, midi skirt, ankle boots. Cozy but intentional.

Six sweaters. Five occasion slots. All visible. All working.

The other three black sweaters? Donated. Their job was done — they taught the system what I actually reach for.

## The Friday Review Loop

Friday 5 PM. Five minutes.

Monday: wore and loved → saved "WFH grocery run"
Tuesday: wore with tweak (swapped boots for loafers at office) → saved "rain commute + office"
Wednesday: skipped (gym clothes all day) → noted "active day needs formula"
Thursday: wore and loved → saved "travel walking day"
Friday: wore and loved → saved "date night"
Saturday: wore with tweak (added light layer) → saved "brunch casual"
Sunday: skipped (loungewear all day) → noted "home day formula"

One real gap flagged: "Zero pieces for Thursday semi-formal travel. Need lightweight rain shell 65-70°F."

Monday morning that gap appeared in my feed with three curated options under $120. Bought the $89 one. Cost-per-wear tracking started automatically.

## What Changed

**43 hours/year** staring at closet → **2 hours/year** (Sunday planning + Friday review)
**$2,800/year** impulse purchases → **near zero** (gaps are data, not feelings)
**Decision fatigue before 8 AM** → **gone**
**Duplicate purchases** → **zero** (the audit caught the third black sweater before I bought it)

The "nothing to wear" panic wasn't a shopping problem. It was a visibility problem. My brain couldn't index what it couldn't see.

Ten minutes. Photo sweep. Retailer sync. Quick tags. Calendar connect. Wear tracking starts.
