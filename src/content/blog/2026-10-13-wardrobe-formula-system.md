Saturday, 10 AM. Coffee. Entire wardrobe on the bed. 112 items.

I wasn't planning a purge. I was planning a rebellion against my own mornings.

## The Breaking Point

Three mornings that week. Three times standing in front of a "organized" closet — shirts with shirts, pants with pants — and drawing a complete blank.

Tuesday: navy blazer, white tee, dark jeans, loafers. Worked. But felt stale.
Wednesday: silk blouse, midi skirt, heels. Too formal for the team standup.
Thursday: sweater, trousers, sneakers. Too casual for the client call.

I had clothes. I had occasions. What I didn't have was a system that translated one into the other in under 30 seconds.

## The Sunday Experiment

I pulled up 28 outfit photos from the past month. Each rated 1–5 on "would wear again."

I grouped the 4s and 5s by *structure* instead of by item.

The pattern was immediate:

- **6 times:** Blazer + neutral top + tailored bottom + polished shoe
- **5 times:** Knit + straight-leg jeans + rotating footwear
- **4 times:** Dress + layer + sneakers/heels
- **3 times:** Statement bottom + quiet top + grounding shoe
- **3 times:** Base + mid-layer + outer + bottom + shoe

Twenty-one of twenty-eight favorites lived in five structures. Five.

I named them: Anchor, Uniform, Dress-Up/Down, Layer Stack, Third Piece.

Then I stress-tested: how many variants can I build *right now* with what I own?

Anchor: 12. Uniform: 18. Dress-Up/Down: 8. Layer Stack: 15. Third Piece: 11.

**Sixty-four outfits. From clothes already in my closet. Zero shopping.**

## The Physical Reorganization

That afternoon, I reorganized the closet by formula zones.

Zone 1 (Anchor): Structured blazers, architectural coats, hero dresses. Quiet supporters folded beneath.
Zone 2 (Uniform): Knits and jeans — same categories, rotating shoes on the rack below.
Zone 3 (Dress-Up/Down): The 4 dresses that actually work both ways. Layers and shoes beside them.
Zone 4 (Layer Stack): Base layers, mid-layers, outerwear, bottoms, shoes — each category in its slot.
Zone 5 (Third Piece): Completers — cardigans, vests, statement necklaces, lightweight blazers.

The "orphan" items — 23 pieces that fit no formula — went to a transition bin. Donate, sell, or tailor decisions for later.

## Monday Morning

7:47 AM. Rain. 61 degrees. Client pitch. Walking commute.

Old me: 15 minutes of "what goes with this?" Decision fatigue bleeding into the 9 AM meeting.

New me: *Tuesday constraints → Formula 1 (Anchor) → Variant C: olive structured blazer + cream silk tee + navy wool trousers + waterproof ankle boots.*

Thirty seconds. Decision made. Out the door by 7:52.

## The Friday Loop

Sunday planning without Friday review is an open loop. Five minutes every Friday:

| Day | Formula | Variant | Rating | Note |
|-----|---------|---------|--------|------|
| Mon | Anchor | C | ✅ | Blazer perfect for pitch |
| Tue | Uniform | A | 🔄 | Boots slipped on wet pavement |
| Wed | Dress-Up/Down | B | ❌ | Meeting went virtual |

Three weeks in, the system knew my real preferences better than any style quiz. Uniform emerged as workhorse — 40% of wears. Dress-Up/Down only 8% — turns out I rarely have mixed-context days.

## The Shopping Shift

Every purchase now answers two questions:

1. Which formula does this strengthen?
2. Which gap does it fill?

The $300 camel coat? Formula 1 hero. Projects 40 wears/year at $7.50/wear. Bought.
The $45 trendy cutout top? Zero formula fit. Returned before it shipped.
