Sunday, 10 AM. Coffee. Entire wardrobe dumped on the bed. 147 items.

I wasn't planning a purge. I was planning a reckoning.

## The Trigger

Three weeks earlier, I bought a $180 cream blazer. "Investment piece," I told myself. "Classic, versatile, forever."

Three weeks. Two wears. One coffee stain. $90 per wear.

Meanwhile, the $40 Uniqlo turtleneck I've worn every Tuesday since January? $0.44 per wear.

The gap between story and reality was embarrassing. So I built the spreadsheet.

## The Method

Column A: Item. Column B: Price paid. Column C: Wears in the last 12 months (honest count, not wishful). Column D: =B/C.

Simple math. Brutal results.

## The Winners (Under $2/wear)

- Black straight-leg jeans ($98, 180 wears) → $0.54
- White cotton turtleneck ($40, 92 wears) → $0.43
- Navy wool blazer ($220, 67 wears) → $3.28
- Camel coat ($380, 41 wears) → $9.27 — *but* it unlocks 15 outfits, so system CPW is lower
- Leather loafers ($165, 140 wears) → $1.18

These are my uniform. The 20% I wear 80% of the time. They earn their keep.

## The Losers (Over $20/wear or ∞)

- Sequined holiday top ($65, 0 wears) → ∞
- Trendy cutout dress ($89, 1 wear) → $89
- "Someday" silk pants ($130, 2 wears) → $65
- Impulse-buy printed shirt ($45, 3 wears) → $15
- Size-6 jeans from 2019 ($120, 0 wears, don't fit) → ∞

Forty-seven items. $3,847. Combined wears in a year: 38.

## The Middle Ground (Where Decisions Live)

- Striped button-down ($75, 12 wears) → $6.25
- Midi skirt ($95, 8 wears) → $11.88
- Chunky knit ($110, 15 wears) → $7.33

These *could* be staples. They're just... not. Not yet. Not with how I actually live.

## What the Numbers Taught Me

### 1. My "Style" Is a Lie I Tell Myself

I think I'm "eclectic, feminine, architectural." My wears say: navy, black, cream, structure, repeat. The sequin top wasn't "aspirational style." It was a costume for a person I don't actually have occasions for.

### 2. Fit Beats Brand Every Time

The $40 turtleneck fits perfectly. The $130 silk pants pull at the hips. I reach for the turtleneck. The pants hang. Price is irrelevant. Fit is destiny.

### 3. Versatility Is a Trap (Unless You Define It)

"Versatile" usually means "goes with nothing specifically." The striped button-down goes with *everything* — which means I never know *what* to wear it with. The navy blazer goes with *these five specific formulas*. I wear it weekly.

Specificity creates wears. Vague versatility creates orphans.

### 4. The Orphans Cost More Than Money

Every morning, my brain processed 147 items. 47 of them were instant "no." That's 47 micro-decisions before 8 AM. Decision fatigue is a tax. I was paying it in mental bandwidth, not dollars.

## The Purge (And What Stayed)

Three bags to donate. One bag to sell. One bag to tailor (the midi skirt — worth saving at $12 alterations).

Ninety-four items remained.

The next morning: 7:47 AM. Rain. Client pitch. I opened the closet. Ninety-four items. Zero hesitation.

Navy blazer. Cream turtleneck. Black jeans. Loafers. Out the door by 7:52.

## The New Buying Rule

Before any purchase over $50, I now ask:

> "At my actual wear rate for this category, how many wears until this hits $3 CPW?"

The $180 blazer needs 60 wears. That's roughly once a week for over a year. Do I have 60 professional occasions in the next twelve months?

If yes, I buy. If no, I don't.

This question has saved me thousands. It's also made me *better* at buying — when I do buy, I know exactly which formula it strengthens, which gap it fills.

## Your Turn

You don't need 147 items. You don't need a Sunday. You need five items, a notes app, and ten minutes.

Pick five things you wore this week. Price ÷ Wears.

Just see what the numbers say.

The math doesn't judge. It just shows you where your money lives — and where it's leaking.
