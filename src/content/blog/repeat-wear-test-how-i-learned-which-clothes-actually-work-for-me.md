I’ve always struggled with the feeling that my closet is full yet I have nothing to wear. Shirts that looked perfect in the store would clash with half my pants once I got home. I kept items “just in case,” forgetting I already owned something similar. Over time, the closet grew, but the daily outfit stress stayed the same.

Last month, I decided to run a repeat wear test. For 30 days, I logged every single item I wore, from socks to jackets. I used a simple notebook, but you could use a notes app or spreadsheet. Each time I put something on, I added a tally mark next to its name. At the end of the month, I had a clear count of how many times each piece saw the light of day.

The results were eye‑obvious. A few favorites showed up 15‑20 times — my go‑to black jeans, a white linen shirt, a versatile blazer. Many items hovered between 2 and 5 wears. Then there was a long tail of things I wore once or not at all. That forgotten sequin top from a holiday party? Zero. The impulse‑buy neon sweater? One wear, then banished to the back shelf.

Seeing the numbers forced me to confront why certain pieces languished. Some were too formal for my casual lifestyle. Others required special care I couldn’t keep up with. A few simply didn’t mix well with the rest of my wardrobe, leaving me stuck with limited combo options. The test didn’t just reveal frequency; it highlighted mismatches between my purchases and my actual routine.

Armed with this data, I did a closet edit. I donated the zero‑wear items and the low‑frequency pieces that no longer sparked joy. I kept the high‑frequency heroes and a few seasonal staples that earn their place during specific months. What remained was a tighter, more intentional collection where every item had proven its worth.

Now, when I shop, I ask myself: Will this piece likely hit double digits in a repeat wear test? If the answer is no, I put it back. This simple mindset shift has cut my impulse buys in half and increased my satisfaction with each new addition. My closet feels lighter, and getting dressed has become a moment of confidence rather than a daily dilemma.

If you’re tired of guessing what works, try the repeat wear test. Track your wears for a month, then let the numbers guide your wardrobe decisions. You’ll finally know what you own and what truly works for you.
