Sunday afternoon. Coffee cold. Entire wardrobe spread across the bed. 134 items.

I wasn't there to purge. I was there because I'd just bought a navy merino sweater — $85 — only to find its near-twin folded in the back drawer. Same weight. Same neckline. Purchased eighteen months apart. The first one had 47 wears. The second? Zero. Still had tags.

That was the third duplicate this year. The rust cardigan (twin of the olive one). The black ankle boots (twin of the brown pair I actually wear). The striped tee (three of them, one worn).

**Three duplicates. $340 this year alone. $3,200 over three years.**

---

## The Visibility Problem

I used to think I had a shopping problem. "I buy too much." "No self-control." "Can't resist a sale."

Turns out I had a **visibility problem**.

When your closet is organized by category — shirts with shirts, pants with pants — you see *items*. You don't see *outfits*. You don't see *patterns*. You definitely don't see *duplicates* hiding in plain sight.

The navy merino wasn't hiding. It was right there with the other sweaters. But my brain filed it under "sweaters I own" not "sweaters that serve the same purpose as the one I wear 47 times a year."

---

## The Audit That Changed Everything

I'd been logging wears in Dripmatiq for six weeks. Not obsessively — just tapping the outfit I wore each morning. Top, bottom, layer, shoes, occasion. Two minutes max.

The app did the rest.

**The dashboard showed me:**
- My top 20 items generated 83% of my outfits
- 42 items had 0–1 wears in 60 days
- 5 formulas covered 91% of my actual rotation
- **11 near-duplicate pairs** — items serving the same formula role with 90%+ overlap

The navy merino twins? Formula 2 (Uniform): neutral knit + dark jeans + white sneakers. The first merino: 47 wears, $1.81/wear. The second: 0 wears, infinite cost per wear.

The rust and olive cardigans? Both Formula 5 (Third Piece): completer over neutral base. Olive: 18 wears, $3.33/wear. Rust: 2 wears, $35/wear.

The black and brown ankle boots? Formula 1 (Anchor) and Formula 3 (Layer Stack). Brown: 31 wears, $4.19/wear. Black: 4 wears, $37.50/wear.

The data didn't judge. It just showed me the math.

---

## What I Did With the Numbers

**Donated: 34 items.** The orphans. The duplicates. The "someday" pieces. Zero regret — the data had already decided.

**Repaired: 3 pieces.** The navy merino (cuff pilling), the brown boots (sole separation), the olive cardigan (snagged elbow). These earned their keep.

**Replaced strategically: 2 items.** A second navy merino (heavier weight for winter). A charcoal blazer (Formula 1 Anchor, current one showing wear). The data wrote the shopping list.

**Archived with dates: 4 pieces.** Interview suit, black-tie dress, wedding guest jumpsuit, funeral coat. Tagged "occasion — review 2027." If unworn by then, they go.

## The Morning After

Now when I open my closet, I see **signal**, not noise.

I know exactly which 20 items pull 83% of the weight. I know my cost per wear on every hanger. I know the 5 formulas that cover 91% of my life.

Getting dressed takes 40 seconds. Not because I have fewer choices — because I have **better** choices. The duplicates are gone. The orphans are gone. The guilt is gone.

And the best part? **I haven't bought a single duplicate in four months.** Every purchase has a thesis. Every item earns its space.
