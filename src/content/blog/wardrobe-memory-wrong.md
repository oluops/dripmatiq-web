I used to do the whole closet purge thing every season. Pull everything out. Try it on. Keep what "sparks joy." Donate the rest.

Felt amazing for about a week.

Then I'd be back at 7:47 AM staring at my closet thinking I had nothing to wear.

The problem wasn't my clothes. It was the audit method.

Traditional audits rely on how things feel in a single try-on session. But feelings lie. Mood distorts. The blazer that feels powerful for a 9 AM presentation feels stiff for weekend brunch. The silk blouse looks gorgeous on the hanger but wrinkles the second you sit down.

You keep things for a fantasy life — events that never happen, weather you don't have, comfort you won't tolerate.

Real clarity comes from data, not feelings.

## The 30-Day Test

I started logging what I actually wore. Quick note each morning: date, items, occasion, comfort 1-10. Under 30 seconds. No editing. Even the "gave up and wore leggings" days.

Two weeks in, patterns emerged that no purge could reveal.

My navy blazer: 18 wears in a month.
Black boots: 22.
White ribbed tank: 15 wears anchoring four different outfit formulas.

These weren't guesses. They were receipts.

The other 95 pieces? 31 never worn. 42 worn once or twice for occasions that never came. 22 worn a few times but wrong sleeve length, slightly tight, annoying fabric.

## The Formulas Took Over

By week 2 I wasn't picking outfits. I was running formulas.

**Formula 1 — Daily Driver:** white tank + jeans + blazer + boots. Work, coffee, school pickup, client call. Swap shoes for occasion.

**Formula 2 — One-Piece Wonder:** black dress + coat + boots. Date, wedding, presentation. Add jewelry to dial formality.

**Formula 3 — WFH Uniform:** cardigan + tank + leggings. Comfort 10/10, camera-ready waist up, zero decision energy.

**Formula 4 — Travel Stack:** tank + jeans + cardigan + blazer + boots worn on plane. Every layer removable, works 55-80°F, fits carry-on.

## The Friday Review Changed Everything

Week 1: 3 loved, 4 tweak.
Week 4: 6 loved, 1 tweak.

The tweaks became a surgical shopping list — not "need a new top" but "need second navy blazer in lighter wool for summer" and "need black loafers with rubber sole for rain."

Bought two things month two. Both filled exact formula gaps. Both hit 10+ wears month three.

## The Question That Changes Everything

The closet question isn't "do I love this."

It's "did I wear this last month? Will I wear it next month?"

If no, it's not wardrobe. It's storage.

You don't need 100 pieces. You need 8-12 that actually work together across your real occasions. The closet that serves your life — not the life you imagine — feels lighter, works harder, and costs less over time.
